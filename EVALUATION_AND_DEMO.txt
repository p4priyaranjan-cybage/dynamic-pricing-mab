PART 2: TECHNICAL EVALUATION QUESTIONS (For Judges/Reviewers)

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION A: ARCHITECTURE & DESIGN DECISIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1: Why did you choose a Contextual Multi-Armed Bandit (CMAB) over deep
    reinforcement learning or a traditional optimization approach?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - CMAB is the right abstraction for this problem: immediate reward per
    decision (book or no-book), no multi-step planning needed
  - Sample-efficient: learns from partial feedback (only the chosen arm's
    reward is observed), unlike supervised learning which needs labels for
    all options
  - Interpretable: each arm has a clear meaning (% price offset)
  - VowpalWabbit's CB_ADF is production-proven, low-latency, and handles
    high-dimensional categorical context well via hashing
  - Deep RL would be overkill (no long-horizon planning needed) and
    sample-hungry (catastrophic in a low-exploration hotel setting)

Q2: Explain the two-level model architecture (PropertyModel + BackboneModel).
    Why not just one model per property or one global model?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Pure per-property: cold-start problem (new hotel has zero data),
    can't generalize from similar hotels
  - Pure global: can't specialize (a luxury NYC hotel prices differently
    from a budget Chicago hotel), tenant isolation violated
  - Solution: BackboneModel (per cluster x tenant) pools signal from
    similar hotels; PropertyModel (per hotel) learns that hotel's quirks
  - Credibility-weighted blend: w = n_observations / (n_observations + k)
    - New property: w~0, mostly follows backbone (pooled signal)
    - Established property: w~1, mostly trusts its own experience
  - k is configurable per-tenant (can opt out of pooling entirely with k~0)

Q3: How do you ensure tenant isolation (e.g., Marriott data never leaks
    into Hyatt models)?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - BackboneModel is scoped per (cluster_id, tenant_id) - SEPARATE VW
    workspaces, separate model files, never share weights
  - PropertyModel is physically separate per property_id (different file)
  - Clusters cut ACROSS the org hierarchy (same market cluster may have
    both Marriott and Hyatt properties), but MODELS within a cluster are
    always tenant-partitioned
  - No database-level cross-tenant query is ever performed in training
  - Config inheritance (extends deep-merge) is per-tenant only

Q4: What is the "scoring_mode" kill-switch and why does it exist?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Three modes: "bandit" (normal), "baseline" (always Base Rate), "shadow"
  - "baseline" mode: one config change in config/tenants/*.yaml instantly
    reverts ALL recommendations to 0% offset (safe default), no code deploy
  - "shadow" mode: bandit scores normally and logs its recommendation, but
    the PUBLISHED price is always the baseline - allows comparing bandit vs
    human without risk. Useful for A/B validation before going live
  - This is the "panic button" for revenue managers if the model behaves
    unexpectedly - zero-code, zero-deploy escape hatch

Q5: Why is the arm ladder asymmetric (-22.5% to +62.5%)?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Downside is bounded: can't price below cost floor, brand minimum, or
    rate parity obligations (OTA contracts)
  - Upside has more room: surge/compression pricing during events can
    legitimately go 50-60%+ above BAR
  - Fine-grained near center (where most data exists): +-6.5% arms capture
    small adjustments without big jumps
  - Cluster-specific scaling via "elasticity_spread" factor: luxury clusters
    (less price-sensitive) get a wider ladder (1.3x), budget clusters may
    get a narrower one

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION B: ALGORITHM & MODEL CORRECTNESS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q6: How does offline pretraining work when historical data has near-zero
    exploration (most logs show the same static price)?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Problem: 85% of historical logs are Base Rate, 10% are one adjacent
    tier, 5% are uniform "pilot test" - can't naively train on this
  - Solution: Fit a REWARD MODEL (XGBoost P(booked) ~ context + offset_pct)
    with a native MONOTONIC CONSTRAINT (higher price -> lower booking prob)
  - Use the reward model to IMPUTE what would have happened at every arm
    for every historical context (counterfactual reasoning)
  - Augmented training: for each historical row, generate a (best_arm,
    worst_arm) example pair using the reward model's predictions
  - Safety nets: premium elasticity floor, distance-based shrinkage,
    doubly-robust correction at the logged arm
  - This is an honest model-based warm-start, NOT claimed to be an
    unbiased off-policy estimator

Q7: What is the "Premium Elasticity Floor" and why is it critical?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Problem discovered empirically: XGBoost's monotone_constraints only
    guarantees DIRECTION (non-increasing), not MAGNITUDE of decline
  - A too-mild fitted P(booked) decline is mechanically dominated by the
    ladder's price growth (2.56x from cheapest to priciest arm)
  - Result: "always pick the most expensive arm" failure mode
  - Fix: For offset_pct > 0, cap P(booked) at a log-odds curve that
    declines at least SEGMENT_ELASTICITY_FLOORS[segment] per unit offset
  - Context-conditioned: corporate travelers (-1.3, less elastic) get a
    milder floor than leisure travelers (-2.6, more elastic)
  - Demand-aware: high-occupancy + events RELAX the floor (guests genuinely
    accept premiums when alternatives are scarce)
  - This makes the imputed reward VARY by context, preventing the flat
    "always discount" training signal that causes arm collapse

Q8: What is the confidence score and how is it computed?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Composite of 3 components, each [0,1], weighted (default 0.4/0.35/0.25):
    * C_sample (0.4 weight):   w = n_observations / (n + k)
      "How much property-specific data exists?"
    * C_agreement (0.35 weight): 1 - normalized stddev across 5 bag members
      "Do the backbone's sub-models agree on the best arm?"
    * C_margin (0.25 weight): gap between top-2 arm probabilities
      "Is the best arm clearly better than alternatives?"
  - Bucketed: Low (<0.4), Medium (0.4-0.7), High (>0.7)
  - Used for: dashboard display AND approval routing (Low confidence ->
    mandatory human approval regardless of price delta)

Q9: What is the doubly-robust correction and why use it?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - For the ONE arm each historical row actually tried, don't just trust
    the reward model's prediction blindly
  - Correct it toward the real observed outcome using inverse-propensity
    weighting: correction = (1/propensity) * (observed - predicted)
  - Propensity is capped (MAX_IPS_WEIGHT=20) to bound variance
  - This is a pragmatic single-arm approximation of full DR (which would
    apply across all arms) - documented honestly as such
  - Result: at the logged arm, the training signal blends model prediction
    with ground truth; at all other arms, it's pure model imputation

Q10: How does VowpalWabbit's online learning work in this context?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - VW uses CB_ADF (Contextual Bandit with Action-Dependent Features)
  - "-q ca" creates quadratic interactions between context (|c) and arm (|a)
    features automatically - so "high occupancy + premium arm" is a learned
    feature combination without manual engineering
  - Online-bagging: 5 independent VW workspaces (BackboneModel), each gets
    Poisson(1)-resampled copies of each example - provides diversity for the
    "agreement" confidence component
  - PropertyModel: single VW workspace, updated immediately on each
    feedback event (fast online loop)
  - BackboneModel: ONLY updated via explicit batch retrain (never from a
    single live event) - prevents one property from instantly influencing
    another's decisions through the shared model

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION C: TRAINING PIPELINE & QUALITY ASSURANCE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q11: Describe the nightly pipeline (run_nightly.py). What are the 3 steps?

ANSWER POINTS:
  Step 1 - RECONCILE: For decisions whose stay_date has passed, simulate
    the realized outcome (booked? cancelled?), compute true_reward (price x
    (1-commission) if booked+not-cancelled, else 0), feed to that property's
    PropertyModel.learn() immediately (fast loop)
  Step 2 - RETRAIN BACKBONES: Rebuild BackboneModels on historical + newly
    reconciled real outcomes. Property models: ONLY pretrain newly-onboarded
    ones (only_missing=True) - never touch established properties' weights
  Step 3 - QUALITY GATE: Run backtest suite on a sample of properties.
    If CI(bandit - baseline) > 0 -> promote new model version
    If not -> ROLLBACK backbones to previous version (property models
    preserved since the gate didn't test them)

Q12: How does the quality gate work? What's the acceptance criterion?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - run_backtest_suite() tests the trained policy against the static
    baseline using the TRUE demand model as an oracle simulator
  - Oracle is NEVER exposed to the bandit itself (only used for evaluation)
  - Runs on a reproducible sample of 6 properties, 150 rounds each
  - Computes mean(bandit_reward - baseline_reward) with bootstrap CI
  - reliably_beats_baseline = True ONLY if the entire CI is above zero
  - If gate fails: automatic rollback of backbone model artifacts to
    their previous timestamped version (model_registry/versioning.py)
  - This prevents a bad retrain from ever reaching production

Q13: How does model versioning and rollback work?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Every save() creates a new timestamped directory: v_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS/
  - A "current.txt" pointer file says which version is active
  - load_or_create() always reads the pointer to find the right version
  - Rollback = update current.txt to point at a previous version tag
  - Automatic pruning: keeps last 5 versions, deletes older ones
  - Backward-compatible: if legacy flat .vw files exist (no pointer),
    loads them directly (migration-friendly)

Q14: What were the key bugs found and fixed during development?

ANSWER POINTS:
  Bug 1 - "Always pick the most expensive arm":
    - Cause: reward model extrapolated a too-weak price decline; reward =
      P(booked) x price was dominated by price growth
    - Fix: Premium elasticity floor (log-odds decline per unit offset)
    - Validated: went from -5318 mean reward gap to -112 (best achieved)

  Bug 2 - "Credibility weight inflated at bootstrap":
    - Cause: PropertyModel.learn() incremented n_observations unconditionally;
      bootstrap called it 3600 times, giving w=0.994 (99.4% self-trust)
      before a single real interaction
    - Fix: count_as_observation=False for bootstrap pretraining
    - Validated: freshly bootstrapped property now correctly shows w=0,
      confidence dropped from false-High (0.985) to honest-Medium (0.575)

  Bug 3 - "Nightly pipeline erased online learning every night":
    - Cause: run_nightly step 2 rebuilt every PropertyModel from scratch,
      saving n_observations=0 - destroyed reconciled learning from step 1
    - Fix: only_missing=True (skip established properties, only pretrain new)
    - Validated: regression test suite tests/test_nightly_learning_preservation.py

  Bug 4 - "Backbone never learned from real bookings":
    - Cause: training only queried is_historical=True (synthetic data);
      live decisions are is_historical=False
    - Fix: query_reconciled_rows() + build_examples_from_reconciled_rows()
      appends real outcomes to training batch alongside oracle-imputed ones
    - Validated: reconciled real rows now appear in backbone training data

SECTION E: FEEDBACK LOOP & LEARNING
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q18: Explain the two-stage reward design (proxy vs true reward).

ANSWER POINTS:
  - PROXY REWARD (fast, same-day): 1.0 if booked, 0.0 if not
    Purpose: quick feedback for the XGBoost reward model's binary label
    Currently only used by offline_eval.py for fitting P(booked)
  - TRUE REWARD (delayed, after stay): price x (1 - commission) if booked
    AND not cancelled, else 0.0
    Purpose: actual revenue signal for online learning (PropertyModel)
    Computed by reward_reconciliation.py once stay_date has passed
  - Why two stages: booking signal is immediate (good for fast iteration),
    but cancellation/no-show only known after the stay (2-30+ days later)
  - Commission matters: OTA booking at $200 nets $170 (15% commission);
    direct booking nets $200. The model should prefer direct-channel pricing

Q19: How does the feedback loop close (from decision to model update)?

ANSWER POINTS:
  1. API makes a decision -> log_decision() -> Decision row in DB
  2. Guest books (or doesn't) -> outcome stored via reconciliation
  3. run_nightly Step 1: for decisions whose stay_date passed,
     simulate_outcome() -> compute true_reward -> PropertyModel.learn()
     (fast online update, immediately improves that property's model)
  4. run_nightly Step 2: backbone retrain now includes reconciled rows
     alongside the original historical dataset (pooled learning)
  5. Quality gate validates the retrained backbone before promotion

Q20: What happens to a decision that a human rejected or overrode?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - REJECTED decisions are NEVER reconciled (reconcile_pending_decisions
    filters on status IN ("approved", "auto_published") only)
  - They had no real-world outcome to learn from (never shown to a guest)
  - OVERRIDDEN decisions (human set a different price): the system
    reconciles using the OVERRIDE PRICE (what the guest actually saw), not
    the bandit's original proposal - but credits the learning to the
    bandit's originally CHOSEN ARM (it learns the consequence of its
    recommendation at the human-adjusted price point)

SECTION F: MULTI-TENANCY & SCALABILITY
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q21: How does the config system work (extends/deep-merge)?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Resolution order: Property > Region > Brand > Chain > Global (_defaults)
  - Each level can override ONLY what it needs (child wins on conflict)
  - Example: Marriott inherits _defaults.yaml (room types, rate plans,
    LOS curve, guardrails), overrides nothing -> gets everything from parent
  - A specific property could override its own guardrails (e.g., tighter
    price bounds for a newly opened hotel) without affecting the fleet
  - Adding a new hotel chain = one YAML file extending _defaults

Q22: How does clustering work and why use (cluster x tenant) scoping?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Clusters = properties competing in similar markets (region x tier for POC)
    E.g., "nyc_midscale_urban", "miami_luxury_resort"
  - Purpose: pool statistical signal from similar properties for better
    learning (especially for cold-start properties with little own data)
  - TENANT SCOPING: within the same cluster, Marriott and Hyatt get
    SEPARATE trained-weight backbones - they pool within their own chain
    but never across chains (antitrust / competitive sensitivity)
  - Production upgrade path: data-driven clustering using comp-set overlap
    + KPI similarity (already has code placeholder: training/clustering.py)
  - Cluster sizing governance: target 3-500 properties per cluster;
    below min -> merge; above max -> split

Q23: How does this scale to 100,000+ properties?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Model count: ~N_clusters x N_tenants backbone models (sub-linear in
    properties) + 1 property model each. Not O(properties^2)
  - Training: each (cluster,tenant) pair is independent -> horizontally
    parallel as separate K8s Jobs or Airflow DAG tasks
  - Storage: SQLite for POC, Postgres for production (same SQLAlchemy code)
  - Model artifacts: local filesystem for POC, object storage (S3/GCS) for
    production with model_registry metadata table
  - Serving: stateless FastAPI pods behind a load balancer; models loaded
    from storage on pod startup. No cross-pod state needed
  - Data residency: per-region deployments; a region's retrain failure
    never touches another region's artifacts

  1. /score runs -> if |price_delta| > 3% OR confidence < 0.4:
     status = "pending_approval" (not auto-published)
  2. Revenue manager sees it in /approval-queue (filterable by property)
  3. Three actions available:
     - Approve: status -> "approved", published to channels via mock publisher
     - Reject:  status -> "rejected", price never shown to guests, never
       reconciled (no learning from an action that never happened)
     - Override: status -> "approved" with override_price set; published at
       the human's price. Reconciliation uses override_price but credits
       the bandit's arm (learns the consequence)
  4. Prior decisions for the same cell are superseded (superseded_at set)

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION H: GUARDRAILS & SAFETY
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q27: How do guardrails prevent the bandit from making bad decisions?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - PRE-DECISION ACTION MASKING: invalid arms are removed BEFORE the bandit
    picks, so exploration never wastes probability on infeasible actions
  - This is fundamentally different from post-hoc rejection (which wastes
    the model's learning budget on rejected actions it can't learn from)
  - Rule registry (adding a new constraint = a small function, no model change):
    * price_bounds: min/max offset clamps
    * competitive_positioning: floor/ceiling vs compset average
    * change_frequency: max 2 publishes/day (throttles channel pushes, not scoring)
    * rate_parity: no-op by construction (single price per cell)
  - GUARANTEE: Base Rate arm (0% offset) ALWAYS survives filtering, even if
    every other arm is excluded. System can never have zero feasible actions

Q28: What happens if ALL guardrails fire and exclude every arm?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Impossible by design: filter_arms() has a fallback - if the allowed
    list is empty after all rules run, it forces the Base Rate arm (0%
    offset) back in. The system ALWAYS has at least one action available
  - This prevents "cannot compute price" errors in production
  - The excluded_arms list in the response shows what was blocked and why

Q29: How is rate parity handled?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Satisfied BY CONSTRUCTION in this POC: exactly one price per
    (property, room_type, rate_plan, LOS, stay_date) cell
  - That same price is pushed to ALL channels (direct + OTA) via the
    publisher layer - no per-channel differentiation possible
  - In production (Phase 9): would need active post-publish verification
    if per-channel pricing were ever introduced
  - The guardrail exists as a documented placeholder for that future case

Q30: How is the system deployed?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - 4 containers via podman-compose (or docker compose):

Q31: How would you move this to production?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Database: SQLite -> Postgres (same SQLAlchemy, just change DATABASE_URL)
  - Model storage: local filesystem -> S3/GCS with model_registry table
  - Orchestration: run_bootstrap/run_nightly scripts -> Airflow DAGs
    (one per cluster for parallel training)
  - Serving: single FastAPI process -> horizontally scaled pods behind ALB
  - Context: synthetic context_builder -> real ETL from PMS/BI feeds
    (same field names as context_schema.yaml - plug-and-play)

  - Monitoring: already Prometheus/Grafana ready; add PagerDuty alerting

Q32: What does the bootstrap pipeline do and why is it idempotent?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Generates synthetic properties from config/tenants/*.yaml
  - Creates historical booking data (synthetic, mirrors real RMS patterns)
  - Trains all backbone and property models from scratch
  - Idempotent check: if DB has properties AND sufficient historical rows
    (>= 100 per property), skips entirely. This handles:
    * Normal re-runs (already complete)
    * Interrupted runs (partial data detected, completes remaining work)
  - FORCE_BOOTSTRAP=1 env var overrides the check (for development)

SECTION J: ADVANCED / CHALLENGE QUESTIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q33: Why VowpalWabbit instead of a custom neural network or PyTorch model?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - VW is purpose-built for contextual bandits (CB_ADF mode)
  - Sub-millisecond inference (critical for real-time pricing APIs)
  - Handles high-dimensional sparse categorical features natively via
    feature hashing (no embedding tables needed)
  - Online learning: updates incrementally without full retraining
  - Proven at scale: used by Microsoft for ad placement (similar problem)
  - "-q ca" auto-generates interaction features between context and arms
  - Deterministic and reproducible given the same random seed

Q34: What's the exploration strategy? How do you balance explore vs exploit?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - VW's CB_ADF with --cb_explore_adf uses epsilon-greedy by default
  - LIVE SERVING: explore=True for real decisions (small epsilon allows
    the model to occasionally try non-greedy arms for learning)
  - BACKTEST/SIMULATION: explore=False (pure greedy, measures policy quality)
  - DRY_RUN (simulate): explore=False (shows the model's best guess)
  - The propensity field is ALWAYS logged (importance-weight correction
    for any off-policy analysis later)
  - Historical data has its own exploration pattern: 85% baseline +
    10% adjacent heuristic + 5% uniform pilot (realistic for hotel RMS)

Q35: What would happen if a competitor chain stole your model artifacts?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - They get a VW binary blob (not human-readable weights)
  - The model is useless without the EXACT same arm ladder, context schema,
    and reference rate composition - all of which are config-driven and
    chain-specific
  - Tenant isolation ensures no cross-chain data ever touches a model file
  - In production: model artifacts in S3 with IAM policies per tenant

Q36: How do you handle new properties (cold start)?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - New property gets a PropertyModel pretrained from its CLUSTER's shared
    reward model (not its own sparse data)
  - n_observations starts at 0 (by design, count_as_observation=False)
  - Credibility weight w = 0 / (0 + k) = 0: 100% backbone-driven initially
  - As real bookings accumulate, w grows and the property gradually
    "graduates" to trusting its own learned preferences
  - Backbone provides reasonable prices from day 1 (learned from similar
    hotels in the same market cluster)
  - This is the empirical Bayes credibility-weighting pattern from
    actuarial science, applied to hotel pricing

Q37: What are the known limitations and what would you fix next?

ANSWER POINTS:
  KNOWN LIMITATIONS:
  - Model does not yet reliably beat static baseline in backtest
    (CI [-174, -58], 3/12 properties win) - documented honestly
  - At least one property shows fully collapsed arm distribution
    (backbone-level issue, not property-level)
  - No same-day learning loop (EnsemblePolicy.record_feedback exists but
    is never called from serving/api.py - all learning is delayed)
  - Synthetic data only (no real PMS integration yet)
  - Single-region deployment (no multi-region failover)

  NEXT STEPS (ordered by expected impact):
  1. Raise PILOT_EXPLORATION_PROB further (0.15 -> 0.25) or use stratified
     exploration (context-aware, not uniform) for richer extreme-arm signal
  2. Context-conditioned premium elasticity floor (partially done: segment-
     aware floors exist; extend to occupancy/event/pace interactions)
  3. More training data per cluster (raise BACKBONE_HISTORY_CAP from 8000)
  4. Retry numeric offset_pct VW arm feature with proper normalization
  5. Implement the same-day fast loop (call record_feedback from /score
     when a rapid booking signal is available)

Q38: How does the demand model work (the "ground truth" oracle)?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Logistic regression: logit = BASE + demand_signals - elasticity*offset
  - Demand signals: occupancy, pace, event_intensity, compset index, pickup
  - Elasticity is segment-specific AND rate-plan-specific:
    * Corporate travelers: 0.55x base elasticity (price-insensitive)
    * Leisure travelers: 1.25x base elasticity (price-sensitive)
    * Government/military plan: 0.7x modifier (captive demand, per diem)
    * Special offer plan: 1.15x modifier (deal-seekers compare aggressively)
  - Cancellation model: base 12%, higher for groups (+5%), lower for
    advance-purchase plans
  - This oracle is NEVER exposed to the bandit engine - only used by:
    (a) run_backtest as evaluation oracle, (b) reward_reconciliation for
    simulating realized outcomes in the POC

Q39: How do you prevent the system from "going rogue" (pricing $1 or $10,000)?

ANSWER POINTS:
  - Layer 1: Arm ladder is DISCRETE and bounded (-22.5% to +62.5% of
    reference rate). The system can ONLY pick from predefined tiers
  - Layer 2: Guardrails exclude arms that violate price bounds or
    competitive positioning rules BEFORE the bandit even sees them
  - Layer 3: Change frequency throttle (max 2/day) prevents rapid thrashing
  - Layer 4: Approval queue catches large deltas (>3%) and low confidence
  - Layer 5: Kill-switch (scoring_mode: baseline) instantly reverts to safe
    Base Rate without code changes or deployments
  - Layer 6: Quality gate blocks bad model versions from being promoted
  - Layer 7: Grafana alerting on arm-distribution collapse
  - TOTAL: 7 independent safety layers, any one of which prevents harm

Q40: What's the reference rate and how is it composed?

ANSWER POINTS:
  ReferenceRate = BAR(property) x RoomTypeMultiplier x RatePlanOffset x LOSCurve

  - BAR: property's base nightly rate (set by revenue team, stored in DB)
  - RoomTypeMultiplier: standard=1.0, deluxe=1.15, suite=1.5
  - RatePlanOffset: BAR=1.0, government=0.75, senior=0.85, special=0.90,
    corporate_negotiated=0.80 (but this one is excluded from bandit)
  - LOSCurve: 1-night=1.0, 2-night=0.98, 3-5=0.95, 6-8=0.90, 9+=0.85

  The bandit's job is ONLY the % offset on top of this reference rate.
  It doesn't reinvent pricing from scratch - it nudges an already-sensible
  base price up or down based on market conditions.

SIMPLE VERSION (for non-technical audience):

  +----------+     +-----------+     +----------+     +----------+
  | Market   | --> | ML Brain  | --> | Safety   | --> | Revenue  |
  | Signals  |     | (Bandit)  |     | Checks   |     | Manager  |
  +----------+     +-----------+     +----------+     +----------+
  occupancy         picks best        guardrails       approves/
  events            price tier        confidence       rejects/
  comp-set          from 9 options    bounds           overrides
  pace/pickup

  THEN -> Published to booking channels (OTA, direct website)
  THEN -> Guest books (or doesn't) -> feedback loops back to ML Brain


TECHNICAL VERSION (for engineering audience):

  Layer 1: DATA
    context_generator -> synthetic BI feed (swap for real ETL in production)
    demand_model -> ground-truth oracle (evaluation only, never seen by bandit)

  Layer 2: TRAINING
    offline_eval.py -> XGBoost reward model (monotonic, context-conditioned)
    train.py -> fleet orchestrator (backbones + property models)
    run_bootstrap.py -> one-time initial training
    run_nightly.py -> reconcile + retrain + quality gate

  Layer 3: SERVING
    FastAPI (serving/api.py) -> all endpoints
    EnsemblePolicy -> credibility-weighted property + backbone blend
    guardrails/constraints.py -> pre-decision action masking
    decision_logger.py -> append-only audit log

  Layer 4: FEEDBACK
    reward_reconciliation.py -> delayed true reward computation
    PropertyModel.learn() -> fast online updates (per property, isolated)
    BackboneModel.learn_batch() -> slow batch updates (per cluster x tenant)

  Layer 5: OBSERVABILITY
    Prometheus -> scrapes /metrics/prometheus every 15s
    Grafana -> pre-provisioned dashboards (zero setup)
    /metrics JSON -> dashboard monitoring tab

  Layer 6: SAFETY
    Guardrails (action masking) -> Approval Queue -> Kill-Switch
    Quality Gate -> Model Versioning -> Automatic Rollback

PART 4: COMMON OBJECTIONS AND RESPONSES
================================================================================

OBJECTION: "But the model doesn't beat the baseline yet!"
RESPONSE:  "Correct - and that's why the quality gate EXISTS. The system is
designed so a model that fails backtest NEVER reaches production. The
architecture IS production-ready (versioning, rollback, kill-switch); the
model tuning is an open item with a documented path to resolution. In a
real deployment, you'd have real (not synthetic) data with richer signal."

OBJECTION: "Why not just use a simple rule-based system?"
RESPONSE:  "Rules can't learn. A rule says 'if occupancy > 80%, raise
price 10%.' But what if comp-set also raised prices? What if it's a
leisure segment that's more price-sensitive? A contextual bandit considers
ALL signals simultaneously and learns the optimal response from data -
including interactions a human rule-writer would never think to specify."

OBJECTION: "This is a black box - revenue managers won't trust it."
RESPONSE:  "Every decision comes with: confidence score, arm probabilities
for all 9 price tiers, which arms were excluded by guardrails and why,
the full context snapshot that drove the decision. Large changes require
human approval. The kill-switch reverts to safe mode in one config change.
This is more transparent than most rule-based systems."

OBJECTION: "How do you handle seasonality / one-off events?"
RESPONSE:  "Context features include occupancy, event_flag, event_intensity,
pace_vs_stly, day_of_week, lead_time. The model doesn't need a separate
'seasonality module' - it LEARNS that high occupancy + weekend + event =
premium pricing is justified, directly from booking outcomes."

OBJECTION: "What about regulatory compliance / rate parity / antitrust?"
RESPONSE:  "Rate parity: by construction (one price per cell, same across
all channels). Antitrust: tenant isolation (Marriott and Hyatt never share
model weights). Regulatory: all decisions are logged with full audit trail,
human approval for large changes, configurable guardrails per jurisdiction."
